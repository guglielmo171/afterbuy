# AfterBuy — Design Direction

## Action before inventory

The interface should make an urgent deadline easy to notice without becoming an alarm dashboard. A user opens AfterBuy to decide whether something needs attention, not to inspect a wall of metrics.

The most urgent item takes the strongest visual position. The rest of the screen provides enough context to make that decision understandable.

## Calm, practical, specific

Neutral surfaces carry ordinary information. Signal colour is reserved for urgency and is always paired with text, dates, and hierarchy. The product should feel precise rather than decorative: dates, prices, state, and the next action are more important than visual flourish.

## Progressive context

The dashboard shows *what* needs attention. A purchase detail provides the *how*: deadline, lifecycle state, notes, references, and support context. This keeps the first screen scannable without hiding the information needed to act.

## Responsive baseline

The product is designed for narrow screens as deliberately as desktop screens. Primary actions must have comfortable touch targets, key urgency must not require horizontal scrolling, and errors or validation messages must remain readable in context.

## Language

UI copy is concise and calm. It explains what happened and offers a next step where one exists. It avoids fear-based wording and does not promise legal protection, perfect data safety, or outcomes the product cannot guarantee.
